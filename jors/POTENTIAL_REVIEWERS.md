# Potential reviewers (for the author's selection)

JORS asks for the names and e-mail addresses of **five** potential reviewers
in the *Comments for the Editor* box. The editor chooses reviewers and is
not obliged to use any suggestion. **No one listed here has been contacted.**

Prepared 27 September 2026 from public web-search results. **No e-mail
address is given**: institutional web pages could not be opened from the
environment used to prepare this file, and an address must never be
inferred from a domain pattern or taken from a third-party aggregator.
Before submitting, choose five candidates, open each *official page* link,
copy the e-mail address exactly as published there, and re-check the
affiliation.

  supervisor of the author and co-author of the GL_q(2|1) source paper.
- **Dr. Sultan A. Çelik**: co-author of the GL_q(2|1) source paper; close
  link to the supervisor.
- Anyone at Yıldız Technical University.
- **Prof. Willem A. de Graaf** (University of Trento): the most directly
  relevant expert in computing with quantum groups, but the author of
  QuaGroup, the software the paper compares itself with. Not proposed; the
  author may add him if they judge this not to be a competing
  interest, stating the relationship to the editor.
- The author has no journal publications and no co-authors, so there are
  no co-authorship conflicts; none of the candidates is known to have any
  professional or personal relationship with the author. The author must
  confirm this and should also check each candidate for joint work with
  S. Çelik or S. A. Çelik (e.g. on zbMATH or MathSciNet).

## Why each is relevant

1. **Scrimshaw** can judge the software against SageMath's quantum-group
   and crystal code from a developer's perspective.
2. **Schilling** combines quantum-algebra expertise with SageMath
   development, including supercrystals relevant to the GL_q(2|1) part.
3. **Thiéry** is a reviewer for software design, sustainability and reuse
   of mathematical research software.
4. **Horn** can assess packaging, testing and positioning relative to GAP
   (where QuaGroup is distributed).
5. **Levandovskyy** can assess the decision to use explicit matrices rather
   than a noncommutative algebra engine, from the perspective of an author
   of such an engine.
6. **Molev** can check the mathematical content of the super R-matrix and
   the graded Yang–Baxter conventions.
7. **Regelskis** can check R-matrix conventions and graded Yang–Baxter
   verification in the superalgebra setting.
8. **Zinn-Justin** can assess the R-matrix and Yang–Baxter verification
   from the integrable-systems side.

## Conflict-screening notes

- Candidates 1–4 are developers of SageMath or GAP, which the paper
  discusses as broader alternatives. This is expertise, not a conflict, but
  the author may prefer not to choose more than two of them.
- Scrimshaw completed his PhD under Schilling (per search results); choosing
  both is acceptable but gives less independent perspectives.
- No candidate is at Yıldız Technical University, and none is known to
  have co-authored with the author or collaborated on this software.

A balanced choice of five, subject to the checks above: Schilling (or
Scrimshaw), Thiéry, Levandovskyy, Molev, Regelskis (or Zinn-Justin).
